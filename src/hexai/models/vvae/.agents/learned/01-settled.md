# Settled

- **The occlusion penalty buys emptiness, exactly as the trap predicted.** Matched 30-epoch pair, onecycle, `--depth_noise 0.08`, gated to unobserved space, weight 1000 (~3% of the loss): `occluded_density` 0.0150 to 0.0009 (-94%) and `invented_density` 0.0227 to 0.0037, so the penalty does what it says. But `lift_agreement` fell 0.739 to 0.521 and `skill_answerable` +0.230 to -0.021, worse than the mean image. `decoded_solid` 0.167 to 0.019 against a `lifted_occupied` of 0.119 in both: the baseline over-fills by 1.4x, the penalised run under-fills by 6.4x. Read `decoded_solid` beside `lifted_occupied` for this, not `invented_density` — an emptied volume wins that one outright.
- **Gating to unobserved space does not keep the penalty off observed geometry.** The charge lands only where the carving saw nothing, yet `lift_agreement` — measured over observed voxels only — still dropped 30%, and diverged inside 5 epochs. The decoder cannot suppress density in one region without it bleeding into its neighbours, so "penalise only the invented mass" is not something a conv decoder can act on locally. Any future volume penalty should expect the same leak.
- **Ungated, the penalty is 6:1 mis-targeted.** Of the density a transmittance-only charge would hit on a trained model, 86% is geometry the carving observed and 14% is invented. Hidden from one query pose is not the same as never observed: seven context frames see 44% of the box, the query renders from one, and everything behind its first surface gets charged. 93% of that charge sits in deep occlusion (T<0.1), so a transmittance dead-zone makes it worse, not better.
- **onecycle beats the old adaptive by +0.23 skill, and the schedule was the whole difference.** Same specs, 200 epochs each, learned lift at 32x60x32: final answerable skill +0.526 against +0.395, last-50 mean +0.503 ± 0.045 against +0.282 ± 0.093, `invented_density` 0.015 against 0.036. onecycle led in all ten 20-epoch blocks.
- **Neither run improved its mean after epoch ~10. 200 epochs bought variance, not skill.** onecycle ep5-25 was +0.525 ± 0.083 against ep181-200 +0.517 ± 0.026 — flat mean, a third the spread; the old adaptive went backwards, +0.372 to +0.282. Training longer is not a lever for this model. Training to a settled rate is: what the tail buys is a checkpoint that reads +0.52 reliably rather than a coin flip between +0.35 and +0.65.
- **`lift_fidelity` does not predict skill.** It decays 0.663 to 0.065 across a onecycle run while skill holds flat — correlation +0.07 over the run, +0.22 after epoch 30, and pooled fidelity bands give a non-monotone mean skill. The learned lift walking away from exact geometry is not what sets the score, in either direction.
- **The whole-frame skill was measuring pixels no gradient ever touched, and every conclusion drawn from it was wrong.** Training with jitter masks the loss to answerable pixels, but the floor was measured over the whole frame, so skill charged the model for the half of it that is never taught. Re-scored, the 300-epoch run climbed +0.377 to +0.618 and plateaued near epoch 200, where the old number said it peaked at epoch 80 and went backwards. The same checkpoint reads -0.904 whole-frame against +0.615 answerable on held-out Office, and -0.000 against +0.779 on a building it trained on.
- **Jitter is what makes the benchmark real.** Without it a window spans ~4 degrees and copying the anchor frame back scores **42.5** against a mean-image 172.9 — four times better, so a memcpy beats anything that does not already render well. With jitter the same copy scores 234.8 against 223.7, slightly worse than the mean image. Every pre-jitter result was measured on a task where doing nothing wins.
- **MSE prefers blur to misplaced structure**, which is why copying loses once the head jitters and why the decoder emits a smooth field. A misplaced edge costs the square of its full contrast; blurring it costs the square of half. Beating both weak baselines — +0.615 against the mean image, +0.633 against copying — is not the same as rendering a scene, and the figures show it is not rendering one.
- **The learned lift is 35% faster than the hard one, once the warp is batched.** 135.7 ms to 87.7 ms per step. Unbatched it was 4.6%: both lifts were Python-loop bound, and every frame shares the voxel centres, so the whole window is one `grid_sample`. The profile that motivated it was also stale — the lift is 82% of the encoder forward but only 33% of a training step, since the render and backward dominate.
- **The unknown channel does separate seen from unseen.** `unknown_gap` +0.701 on held-out Office, where any constant scores 0. `unknown_mae` 0.084 could not show that, since ~88% of the box is unobserved and guessing so scores well.
- **Three buildings fix scene-corr.** 160 epochs over Hospital, House and HQWesternSaloon took it 0.736 to 0.39 and the generic-room collapse is gone. Cross-building generalisation is real but modest: +0.779 answerable on a building it trained on against +0.615 on one it never saw, so more buildings is a lever, not the lever.

- **jitter and unknown only work together.** Alone, `unknown` scored *below* baseline on agreement (0.720 against 0.795) and `jitter` barely moved the fill. Together the decoded volume went from 58% solid to **10.1%**, against a lifted 10.0% — the first run whose geometry matched what was observed. Wider viewpoints are what make the machinery for representing ignorance worth carrying.
- **That run had plateaued by its midpoint.** The second half of 80 epochs bought ~1% on `recon_answerable` and nothing on agreement. Not undertrained, so more epochs is not the lever — one house is.

- **depth beats rgb** as the render target — 0.386 mean scene-corr against 0.454. Depth needs no appearance channel at all, so the volume drops from 4 channels to 1 and the latent stops paying to remember colour.
- **mse beats an edge-aware loss** (0.277 against 0.388) even on depth, where the gradient term should have helped. The edge loss has been removed. That was scene-corr on a pre-jitter task, and mse is what drives the decoder to blur, so this is the first thing to retest if structure is the goal.
- **onecycle beats the alternatives** — it led on skill in all four of its runs against `adaptive` and against a fixed cosine-with-restarts schedule since deleted. Best rate ~3e-3, well above the 1.3e-3 we assumed for a while. Scored on the whole-frame skill, so worth one re-run before trusting it.
- **Learning rate has a narrow window.** 2e-4 collapses, 1e-3 works, 3e-3 collapses as a constant — but survives as a onecycle *peak*, because warmup is what stops the early blowup.
- **A single global beta collapses the latent.** 0.05 takes scene-corr from 0.277 to 0.750 at the same rate (same reference run as the loss comparison). Below ~1e-3 is safe.
- **Jitter always implies masking.** Aiming the head lifts query viewpoint spread from 3.7° to 21.4°, but drops the answerable fraction from 77% to 58% — without the mask, two fifths of the loss would score pixels no context frame saw.
- **The robot's fov is 49°, not a hyperparameter.** `pixel_to_camera_ray` derives focal length from it, so a wrong fovy puts every unprojected point at the wrong angle. Seven frames restores the coverage a 90° camera got from four.
